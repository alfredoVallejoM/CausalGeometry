# CA-22 — noncommutative localization and general local arithmetic closure

Date: 2026-09-26

CA-22 starts from the arithmetic already present in CA-18.  It must not
reimplement residual arithmetic, p-adic completion or the q=p^f branching
theorems that are already on main.

The remaining mathematical gap is narrower and harder:

1. construct certified Ore quotient calculi from intrinsic hypotheses instead
   of accepting the quotient relation/coherence as input;
2. prove the expected universal properties;
3. derive left/right comparison under explicit two-sided hypotheses;
4. close genuinely nonclassical factorization questions;
5. identify the abstract q+1 projective tree with a general rank-two local-field
   lattice tree, not only with the concrete Q_p regression.

## 1. Existing source boundary

Already implemented:

- RightOreSquare / LeftOreSquare;
- RightOreCondition / LeftOreCondition;
- raw right and left fractions;
- certified RightFractionCalculus and LeftFractionCalculus;
- quotient carriers once a certified calculus is supplied;
- monoid structures on those quotients;
- source embeddings and chosen denominator inverses;
- target GroupRealization and uniqueness from the source restriction;
- commutative localization;
- central-denominator localization;
- central bilateral comparison;
- residual/localization exact-division comparison;
- general prime-power towers q=p^f;
- DVR residue branching contract;
- projective sphere cardinal (q+1)q^n;
- q-element reduction fibers;
- universal projective Bruhat--Tits graph;
- proof that every universal-tree vertex has outgoing degree q+1;
- concrete p-adic rank-two lattice rays and complete P1(F_p) local stars;
- transport of the p-adic star classification to every lattice representative.

Therefore the phrase "generalize from p to q=p^f" is no longer an accurate
description of the open work.  q=p^f is already represented in the abstract
local-ring/projective layer.

## 2. Genuine Ore quotient construction

The current RightFractionCalculus intentionally requires:

- a relation on right-fraction presentations;
- proof that it is an equivalence;
- representative multiplication;
- congruence of multiplication;
- unit/associativity coherence;
- denominator inverse laws;
- normal forms.

That is correct as a safe interface, but it means RightOreCondition alone has
not yet been shown to generate such a calculus.

CA-22 must state the exact additional hypotheses.  In classical Ore
localization this normally includes a reversibility/cancellation condition in
addition to common multiples.

The implementation must not prove a false theorem of the form

RightOreCondition => localization

without the extra hypothesis needed to make the presentation relation
transitive and multiplication well-defined.

The same applies independently on the left.

## 2A. Canonical mathlib Ore bridge

The repository now uses the existing mathlib Ore localization as the certified
construction instead of duplicating its delicate quotient proof.

For the left orientation, an instance

`OreLocalization.OreSet S`

supplies both:

- the left Ore square used by `LeftOreSquare`;
- weak right cancellation/reversibility.

`MathlibOreBridge.lean` maps raw `LeftFraction` presentations into the
canonical quotient, proves selected denominators are units, proves agreement
with the pre-existing group semantics, and exports the universal group
morphism plus uniqueness.

For the right orientation the source uses `S.op` in the opposite monoid.
`MathlibRightOreBridge.lean` proves that the left Ore equation in the
opposite monoid unops to the required `RightOreSquare`, constructs the
canonical right localization as the opposite of that left localization, and
recovers the expected group semantics (f(a)f(s)^{-1}).

This closes the canonical quotient/universal-property problem under a
standard certified OreSet hypothesis without claiming that the weaker raw
`RightOreCondition` or `LeftOreCondition` alone is sufficient.

## 3. Universal property

For a certified quotient Q and a target group G, the desired theorem is:

for every monoid morphism f : A →* G sending every selected denominator to a
unit (automatic in a group target), there exists a unique morphism

f~ : Q →* G

such that

f~ ∘ i = f.

The repository already proves the uniqueness half for a supplied
GroupRealization and proves complete universal properties in the commutative
and central-denominator sectors.

CA-22 must derive the general Ore realization from the canonical quotient
construction rather than requiring realization coherence as additional data.

## 4. Left/right comparison

Right and left localizations must remain different until a theorem constructs

Q_R ≃* Q_L.

The current BilateralFractionComparison is a certificate of such an
equivalence; it does not assert that two Ore conditions are sufficient.

The closure theorem must name exact two-sided hypotheses under which the
certificate is constructible and prove preservation of

- source embedding;
- denominator inverses;
- right normal forms;
- left normal forms.

## 4A. Central cancellative specialization

`CentralOreMathlib.lean` now proves that central denominators in a
`CancelMonoid` satisfy the full mathlib `OreSet` hypothesis.

The two ingredients are kept visible:

- centrality supplies the Ore rewrite;
- cancellativity supplies the weak reversibility/cancellation witness.

The construction is also transported to `S.op`, so both canonical left and
right localizations are available in the same central noncommutative sector.

The chosen Ore numerator/denominator reduce definitionally to the expected
central witnesses.  What remains for CA-22.18 is a theorem identifying the
older project-local certified quotient calculi with these canonical
localizations; semantic agreement alone is weaker than a monoid equivalence.

## 5. Residuation versus localization

The existing source already proves an important boundary:

inverse multiplication in the localization agrees with residual division
exactly on the divisible/exact domain.

Outside that domain residuation is an order-theoretic approximation and no
localization equality is claimed.

CA-22 must preserve this distinction.

A useful closure theorem is to identify the maximal source subdomain on which
the backward structural map induced by denominator inversion agrees with the
residual.

## 6. General local field lattice realization

The abstract DVR/projective tower now has the right q+1 branching law.

The concrete p-adic lattice realization proves the expected P1(F_p) star.

What is still missing is a theorem parameterized by a sufficiently complete
discretely valued field/local PID setup that constructs:

- rank-two lattices;
- homothety classes;
- index/residue adjacency;
- P1(k)-classified neighbors;
- the lattice graph;
- an equivalence with the abstract projective universal tree.

That is the actual general q=p^f local-field closure.

# Exit criteria

CA-22 closes only when the generic right and left localization quotients are
constructed from intrinsic hypotheses, their universal properties are proved,
and the general supported local-field lattice tree is compared to the existing
abstract projective tree.

The central and commutative sectors are regressions, not substitutes for that
closure.
